# Proof Narrative: Napoleon Bonaparte stood shorter than the average Frenchman of his era.

## Verdict

**Verdict: DISPROVED (with unverified citations)**

Napoleon Bonaparte was not short — he was actually taller than the average Frenchman of his time, and the claim to the contrary is one of history's most persistent myths.

## What was claimed?

The claim is that Napoleon Bonaparte was below average height for a French man living in his era. This idea is deeply embedded in popular culture, where "Napoleon complex" has become shorthand for short-man overcompensation. The claim matters because it shapes how we understand Napoleon's personality, his ambitions, and his relationships with those around him.

## What did we find?

Multiple independent sources agree on Napoleon's actual height: approximately 1.67 to 1.68 meters, or just under 5 feet 7 inches in modern terms. This figure comes from contemporary accounts — his valet, a general who served with him, and his personal physician all recorded him at just over "5 pieds 2 pouces" in the French measurement system of the time. Encyclopaedia Britannica corroborates this, placing him at roughly 1.68 meters at the time of his death.

The average height of French men during Napoleon's lifetime (roughly 1800–1820) was about 164 to 165 centimeters — around 5 feet 4 to 5 feet 5 inches. Britannica puts the average at approximately 1.65 meters, and historical anthropometric records from the Napoleon Series place it at 164.1 centimeters. These two independent estimates agree within about a centimeter of each other.

Putting the numbers together: even using Napoleon's lower reported height (1.67 meters) and the highest reported average for French men (1.65 meters), Napoleon still stood about 2 centimeters taller than the typical Frenchman of his day. He was not below average — he was above it.

So where did the myth come from? The origin is a measurement confusion. Napoleon's height was recorded in French units, where an "inch" (pouce) was slightly longer than an English inch — 2.71 centimeters versus 2.54. When British sources converted his height using English inches, they got roughly 157 centimeters, or 5 feet 2 inches. That error, combined with British wartime propaganda cartoons by caricaturist James Gillray, cemented the image of a diminutive dictator. The caricature was political, not accurate.

## What should you keep in mind?

The "with unverified citations" qualifier in the verdict reflects that two of the four sources used had quote-matching limitations during automated verification — not that the underlying facts are in doubt. The actual height figures (1.68 meters, 1.67 meters, 1.65 meters) were all confirmed on the live source pages. Two of the sources, History.com and The Napoleon Series, are editorially credible but were flagged as lower-tier by the automated credibility system simply because their domains weren't pre-classified.

The disproof holds even if you set those two sources aside entirely: Britannica alone confirms Napoleon at ~1.68 meters and typical French men ranging from 1.58 to 1.68 meters, placing Napoleon at the upper end of that range.

One genuine limitation: height data from this era comes from military conscription records, which tend to underrepresent the tallest men (officers were often exempt). This could slightly inflate the reported "average," making Napoleon's advantage over the mean slightly smaller — but it does not come close to reversing the finding.
